---
title: Second-Order Thinking
author: Farnam Street
url: https://fs.blog/second-order-thinking/
---

Most people stop at first-order thinking: what happens immediately after a decision.
Second-order thinking asks the next question — and then what? It's the discipline of
following consequences past the point where they stop being obvious.

The reason it's rare isn't that it's hard to understand. It's that first-order answers
are available instantly and feel complete. Second-order answers require holding a
decision open long enough to trace where it leads, which is uncomfortable when a
decision is already 80% made.

Compounding is where this matters most. A choice with a small negative second-order
effect, repeated, dominates the first-order gain that motivated it — which is why
investors who optimise for this quarter's return so reliably lose to ones who don't.
