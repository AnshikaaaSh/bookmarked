CHAPTER I

The house on Aldergate had been empty for a year before Amara moved in. She spent the
first week doing nothing but opening windows, as if the rooms had been holding their
breath and she meant to let them out.

Her neighbour, an older woman named Mira, watched from the porch opposite and said
nothing for four days. On the fifth, she crossed the street with a plate of something
covered in foil and introduced herself as the person who had complained about the
previous tenant.

CHAPTER II

Mira had taught for thirty years and had not entirely stopped. She corrected Amara's
grip on the pruning shears, her pronunciation of the street's name, and eventually her
assumption that the garden had ever been anything but deliberate.

"Everything in it was planted by someone who knew exactly how tall it would get,"
she said. "That's the difference between a garden and a yard."

CHAPTER III

Dev arrived in October, unannounced, with the particular confidence of someone who had
known Amara since they were seven and had never once called ahead. He took in the
house, the garden, and Mira on her porch, and pronounced the whole arrangement
suspicious.

Amara pointed out that he had driven four hours to say so.

CHAPTER IV

The letter came addressed to the previous tenant. Mira recognised the handwriting
before Amara had finished reading the envelope, and for the first time since they had
met, she went back inside without saying goodbye.
