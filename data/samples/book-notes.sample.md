## Chapter 1

- A system is more than the sum of its parts: it's elements, interconnections, and a
  purpose. Change the interconnections and you often change behaviour more than by
  swapping any single element.
- Stocks are the memory of a system — the accumulated history of its flows.

## Chapter 2

- Feedback loops are where behaviour comes from. A balancing loop seeks a goal; a
  reinforcing loop compounds whatever is already happening.
- Second-order thinking means tracing a decision's ripple effects through those loops
  before acting, rather than reacting to the first visible consequence.
- Delays in feedback loops are the single most common cause of oscillation — the
  system overshoots because information about the effect arrives after the cause.

## Chapter 6

- Leverage points are places to intervene. The counterintuitive finding is that the
  obvious levers (parameters, subsidies, taxes) are the weakest, and the strongest is
  the paradigm the system arises from.
